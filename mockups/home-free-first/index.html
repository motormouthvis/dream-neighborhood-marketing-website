Note 8 · Pricing claim now sourced — and it moved in our favor Checked against the GreatSchools NearbySchools API page on 13 Aug 2026. Their published rates are $52.50/mo for School Essentials and $97.50/mo for School Quality, both including 15,000 calls, then metered at $0.003 and $0.006 per call up to a 300,000 ceiling. So your $95 floor was very close to right, and the $800 top end was too low: School Quality at the ceiling is $1,807.50/mo, and LocalLogic runs to $1,500+. The copy now reads $97.50 to $1,800 per month, which is defensible at both ends and no longer contradicts the LocalLogic figure.

On comparing against the ratings tier: agreed, and the table now leads with School Quality and drops School Essentials, since Essentials has no ratings at all and muddied the point. But I could not find an $87.50 tier anywhere on their pricing page. The only two published prices are $52.50 and $97.50, and I pulled those from the raw page markup rather than the rendered text, because the cents are superscripted and scrape as “$5250” and “$9750”. I have used $97.50. If $87.50 came from a quote or an older rate card, tell me and I will switch it, but I did not want to publish a number I cannot point at a source for.

Worth noting the comparison is even better than a price gap: their $97.50 tier explicitly excludes the 1–10 ratings and assigned schools. Getting real ratings from GreatSchools means their separate Data Licensing product, which has no public pricing at all. So we are not just cheaper than their top published tier, we include the thing it does not sell.
Note 9 · Unlimited free views is a bigger weapon than it looks Confirmed no view cap on the School Explorer, so unlimited views is now stated in the hero section copy, the free product card, the pricing grid and the comparison table. It is worth hammering, because GreatSchools meters everything: their $97.50 tier includes 15,000 lookups and then charges $0.006 for every one after, to a 300,000 ceiling. Unlimited versus metered is a cleaner contrast than free versus paid, and it kills the “what happens when my traffic grows” objection outright.

One thing to settle before this ships: the paid tiers directly below still cap Solo at 5,000 views and Brokerages at 25,000, so the grid currently reads free = unlimited, paid = capped. A prospect will ask why paying gets them less. It is defensible — the Neighborhood Explorer has real per-lookup cost behind it while the school data does not — but the page does not say that anywhere, and right now the caps are the first thing you see on the paid cards. Either raise them, describe them as fair-use, or add one line of explanation.
