Note 7 · Counting data points against Local Logic You can absolutely reach a very big number, and the four tiles below are the honest version of it: every figure is one you already gave me. Before we print a headline total, decide the counting rule and write it down, because a number this size will get challenged. Three defensible methods, from most conservative to most aggressive:

1. Records. 16M points of interest + 119K schools + census geographies. Lands in the tens of millions and nobody can argue with it.
2. Records × attributes. If each POI carries name, category, coordinates, address and distance, 16M POIs alone becomes 80M+ values. Schools at roughly 20 metrics each add another 2.4M.
3. Data points served per address. 38+ per address across every US address we can resolve. That is the method that produces a billions-scale number, and it is the one Local Logic almost certainly uses, because that is how you get to “x million data points” on a slide.

My recommendation is to lead with the concrete counts, not the headline total. 16 million points of interest is more persuasive than 2 billion data points, because the first one sounds like a database and the second one sounds like marketing. Keep the big number as the follow-up for anyone who asks how we compare to Local Logic, with the counting rule ready. Give me their exact claim and the number you want to stand behind and I will put it on the competition page.

Note 9 · How to spin the caps Your real reason is sound and more common than you think: the view allowance is how you stop a 200-agent brokerage buying a $24.92 solo seat. Traffic is a proxy for company size, and using it means you can keep the genuinely attractive part of the offer, which is unlimited agents, no per-seat pricing. Almost every usage priced SaaS works this way. Three moves make it land.

Never call it a cap or a limit. The copy above says included, and the plan cards now read “5,000 listing views included monthly” instead of “up to 5,000 views per month.” Same number, and an allowance sounds like something you were given rather than something you are being denied. GreatSchools words it exactly this way.

Explain the asymmetry before they notice it. Free is unlimited and paid is not, which looks backwards until you say why. The one-line explanation above turns that into proof the free offer is real: we are not capping the free product because it genuinely costs us nothing, and we are metering the paid one because it genuinely costs us something. That is a far better answer than silence, and it makes the “free for life” claim more believable rather than less.

Remove the fear of an overage bill. “We will never switch you off mid-month” costs you nothing and kills the objection that a busy month produces a surprise invoice or a dead widget in front of a buyer. Note that this is a promise, so make sure the billing system actually behaves that way.

What I would not do is defend the number on the page. Nobody argues with an allowance they have not hit yet. If a big brokerage does the math and asks, that is a sales conversation you want, because it means they are large enough for the Brokerages or Partners tier.
